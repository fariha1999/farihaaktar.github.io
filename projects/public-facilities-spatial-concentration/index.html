Spatial Concentration of Public Facilities in the Districts of Bangladesh

A district-level regional planning project examining how educational, health and community facilities are distributed across Bangladesh, where facility deficits and concentrations occur, and how regional disparities can be identified using spatial and statistical techniques.

Objective Explore the spatial concentration of selected public facilities across all 64 districts, identify deficient and concentrated regions, and support more balanced regional planning.
Study area 64 districts of Bangladesh
Facilities Primary & Secondary Schools · Colleges · Private Hospitals/Clinics · Government Health Complexes · Growth Centers · Rural Markets
Methods & tools Location Quotient · Weighted Index · Gini Index · Pearson Correlation · Threshold Population · ArcMap 10.8
Project outputs

Project context

Measuring regional inequality in access to public facilities

Planning problem

Public facilities are unevenly distributed across Bangladesh. Population growth and uneven urbanization can create both deficits and concentrations, increasing pressure on facilities in some districts while leaving other regions underserved.

Analytical scope

The project analyzed seven educational, health and community-facility groups using district population and facility counts, then compared concentration, inequality, regional status and estimated facility requirements.

Methodology

Spatial concentration, inequality and regional classification

01 Location Quotient

Each facility was compared with district population and the national facility-population ratio to identify deficiency or concentration.

02 Weighted Index

The seven facility LQ values were combined to generate a composite score and delineate formal facility regions.

03 Gini & Lorenz analysis

Gini coefficients and Lorenz curves were used to compare spatial inequality among different facility types.

04 Correlation & thresholds

Pearson tests assessed population-facility relationships, while population thresholds estimated required educational facilities.

Key findings

Facility deficits differ strongly by sector and region

Regionalization
9.38% Districts classified highly deficient

Dhaka, Gazipur, Narayanganj, Brahmanbaria, Lakshmipur and Cox's Bazar fell in the highly deficient composite region.

Spatial inequality
0.57 / 0.53 Highest Gini values

Private hospitals/clinics and government health complexes showed the greatest spatial inequality among the selected facilities.

Education
Dhaka · Narayanganj · Cox's Bazar Primary & secondary school deficit

These districts were identified as highly deficient in both primary and secondary school provision relative to population.

Correlation
6 of 7 Population relationships significant

Population was significantly related to six facility groups; government health complex was the exception in the Pearson test.

Study limitation

Facility and population data were based on BBS 2011 district statistics. The project therefore represents the spatial pattern supported by that dataset and may not reflect the most recent distribution of facilities.

Threshold planning

Estimating additional educational facility requirements

Primary school threshold 5,000 people Cox's Bazar required the largest proportional increase in primary schools: 86%.
Secondary school threshold 7,000 people Dhaka required the highest additional secondary-school provision in the study.
College threshold 52,600 people Feni showed the highest estimated requirement for additional colleges.
Planning implications

Using facility deficits to guide regional investment

01 · Education Plan required schools & colleges

Integrate identified facility requirements into local and municipal planning where education provision is inadequate.

02 · Health Prioritize deficient health regions

Use the mapped deficits to guide additional private hospitals, clinics and government health complexes.

03 · Local economy Strengthen growth centers

Target districts with lower provision of growth centers to support regional economic development.

04 · Rural access Connect markets & growth centers

Improve road and infrastructure connections to rural markets and growth centers, particularly in deficient regions.

05 · Regional targeting Use formal regions for investment

Direct greater planning attention and public investment toward highly and moderately deficient districts.

06 · Equity Reduce health-service inequality

Address the especially uneven spatial distribution of private hospitals and government health complexes.

07 · Population pressure Match facilities to demand

Use population-based thresholds alongside LQ results when evaluating educational facility requirements.

08 · Monitoring Update the facility database

Repeat the analysis with newer district-level data to track changes in regional facility concentration over time.

Project outcome

The project combined facility mapping, Location Quotient analysis, formal regionalization, inequality measures and population-based demand assessment to identify districts requiring greater attention in education, health and community infrastructure planning.
