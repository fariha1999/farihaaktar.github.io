A Study on Informal Market at the Local Level and the Role of Stakeholders

A participatory planning study of the informal market beside Iraqi Playground in Azimpur, Dhaka, examining its spatial and temporal characteristics, vendor and customer problems, stakeholder relationships, and locally grounded planning interventions.

Objective Understand the market's spatial and temporal extent, identify problems experienced by vendors and users, and explore the roles, interactions and influence of stakeholders at the local level.
Study area Iraqi Playground · New Paltan Lane · Azimpur, Dhaka
Market profile 22 vendors · Regular morning market · Approx. 7:30 AM-1:00 PM
Methods PRA · FGD · In-depth Interviews · Social/Resource Mapping · Transect Walk · Seasonal Analysis · Pairwise Ranking · Venn Diagram · SWOT
Participatory outputs
Spatial, Temporal & Stakeholder Evidence

Project context

Understanding an informal market through local knowledge

Market setting

The market operates beside Iraqi Playground in Azimpur and includes vegetable, fruit, fish, chicken, spice, clothing and utensil vendors using carts, vans and temporary ground-based structures.

Planning perspective

Instead of treating informal vending only as a spatial encroachment issue, the project examined everyday routines, infrastructure deficiencies, vendor rights, customer concerns and stakeholder influence.

Participatory methodology

Combining space, time and relationship-based PRA tools

01 Spatial understanding

Social/resource mapping, natural-resource mapping and transect walks documented physical conditions, facilities and spatial problems.

02 Temporal analysis

Historical timeline, seasonal diagrams, weekly/occasional variation and daily schedules captured changes in market activity over time.

03 Problem prioritization

Pairwise ranking and cause-effect diagrams were used with vendors and residents to identify and explain priority problems.

04 Stakeholder analysis

Venn diagrams, process maps, interviews and SWOT analysis explored influence, responsibilities, conflicts and implementation opportunities.

Key findings

What the participatory assessment revealed

Space & infrastructure
Good local access, weak basic facilities

The market benefits from nearby residential customers and WASA water access, but lacks sanitation, drainage, storage and formal waste facilities.

Vendor security
Informal tenure shapes everyday risk

Without formal rights to the space, vendors reported concerns including eviction, extortion, space conflict and restricted business hours.

Temporal pattern
Livelihood conditions vary by season

Vegetable, fruit and fish vendors experience different seasonal sales patterns, while rainfall, waterlogging and heat disrupt operations.

Stakeholders
Local institutions strongly influence operations

Ward-level actors, DSCC, the wholesale market and other local stakeholders shape management, water, waste, trading and market continuity.

Study limitations

Vendors had limited time for participatory sessions; sensitive information on extortion was difficult to obtain; local political leaders were not directly approached on that issue; and irregular attendance limited participation of the market's only female vendor in some FGD sessions.

Planning recommendations

From participatory evidence to local interventions

01 Local vending committee

Create a ward-level body to represent vendors and improve communication with DSCC and local authorities.

02 Registration & ID

Introduce transparent vendor registration, identity cards and locally administered licensing arrangements.

03 Infrastructure & utilities

Provide waste bins, sanitation, drainage, storage, shelter, water and other essential facilities within the market layout.

04 Participatory vending zones

Define vending areas and internal zones for wet and semi-wet products using the vendors' dream map and site requirements.

05 Price transparency

Display regularly updated price charts and strengthen monitoring to address customer concerns over price hikes.

Project outcome

The project combined participatory spatial mapping, temporal analysis, problem prioritization and stakeholder analysis to develop recommendations that address both the physical design of the market and its governance, vendor security and local management.
