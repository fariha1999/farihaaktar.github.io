Resilient River Basin: Landscape Planning of the Jamuna River Basin

An ecology-based landscape planning project for a selected part of the Brahmaputra-Jamuna River Basin, integrating topography, hydrology, geology, biodiversity, infrastructure and settlement analysis into a phased planning framework for environmental conservation and resilient local development.

Objective Analyze how existing development affects the river-basin ecology, identify key pressures on natural systems, and guide future planning strategies that conserve the environment and biodiversity.
Study area 5 × 5 km · Pogaldigha Union · Sarishabari Upazila · Jamalpur
Planning horizon 2021-2050 · Short, middle and long-term phases
Methods & tools Google Earth Pro · AutoCAD · Adobe Illustrator · Mapping · SWOT · Literature Review · Case Study · Iterative Strategy Development

Project context

Planning a dynamic river landscape as an ecological system

Landscape setting

The selected site is part of the Brahmaputra-Jamuna floodplain, where livelihoods, agriculture, fisheries and settlement are closely linked with seasonal river processes. Normal flooding supports the floodplain economy, while severe floods and bank erosion create substantial risk.

Planning challenge

Infrastructure on the floodplain, embankments, industrial pollution, dispersed settlement, biodiversity loss and changing land use were treated as interconnected ecological and planning issues rather than isolated problems.

Methodology

From landscape diagnosis to iterative strategy development

01 Site selection & mapping

A 5 × 5 km site was selected and mapped for topography, geology, hydrology, biodiversity, infrastructure and settlement conditions.

02 SWOT & precedent review

Site strengths and threats were examined alongside the Garden City concept and river-based planning precedents.

03 Comprehensive landscape study

Natural systems and human interventions were analyzed together to identify ecological conflicts and development pressures.

04 Iterative planning strategies

Planning proposals were repeatedly reviewed and refined before being organized into a three-phase implementation framework.

Key issues

Pressures identified across the river-basin landscape

Floodplain
Infrastructure disrupts natural processes

Embankments, paved roads, railway and highway sections on the floodplain alter natural flow, siltation and ecosystem functions.

Pollution
Industrial activity affects river ecology

The fertilizer industry was identified as a source of environmental pressure, leading to proposals for ETP, discharge ponds and vegetated buffers.

Settlement
Dispersed growth complicates services

Scattered and unplanned linear settlements make community facilities and utilities more difficult and costly to provide.

Ecology
Biodiversity and green space are under pressure

Habitat loss, vegetation removal, overfishing and river interventions threaten flora, fish, dolphins, gharials and other fauna.

Planning strategy

Five integrated aspects of the resilient river-basin plan

01 · Nature conservation Restore the floodplain

Remove or relocate damaging infrastructure, exclude embankments, establish river-edge buffers and transform industry toward cleaner operation.

02 · Land use Cluster future settlement

Relocate development from sensitive floodplain areas, strengthen clustered settlement and provide a more efficient network of community facilities.

03 · Transportation Adapt mobility to the river

Expand access roads outside sensitive areas, use boats as an alternative mode and relocate highway/rail sections away from the floodplain over time.

04 · Ecology & agriculture Protect habitat and productive land

Create greenways and sanctuaries while promoting crop rotation, polyculture, composting and flood-tolerant agricultural practices.

05 · Energy Transition toward renewables

Introduce solar panels and biogas plants in phases, with the project targeting progressively higher renewable-energy coverage toward 2050.

Implementation

A 30-year phased planning scheme

Short term 2021-2025 Begin floodplain restoration, introduce initial buffers, renewable energy, access improvements and early settlement interventions.
Middle term 2026-2035 Expand green networks, access infrastructure, renewable energy coverage, ecological restoration and community facilities.
Long term 2036-2050 Complete major relocation and restoration measures and consolidate the integrated landscape-planning framework.
Study limitations

The project relied primarily on secondary information and Google Earth Pro because a physical field survey was not possible within the available time and cost. Image resolution limited identification of some site features, some exact land uses were uncertain, and site-specific published information was limited.

Project outcome

The project produced an ecology-based, three-phase landscape plan for the selected Jamuna River Basin site. It links floodplain conservation, clustered settlement, adaptive transportation, biodiversity protection, renewable energy and resilient agriculture within one long-term spatial framework.
