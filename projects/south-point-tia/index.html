Traffic Impact Assessment (TIA) of South Point School and College

A transportation planning project assessing how South Point School and College affects traffic operations, pedestrian movement and road safety around Malibagh Chowdhury Para, and developing site-specific measures to reduce school-related traffic impacts.

Objective Assess existing road and traffic conditions around the school, identify problems through field surveys and stakeholder input, and propose practical interventions for safer and more efficient access.
Study area South Point School & College · Malibagh Chowdhury Para · Ward 23, DNCC
Fieldwork Reconnaissance · Traffic Survey · Pedestrian Survey · Stakeholder Interviews
Analysis Traffic Volume · Mode Share · Peak-Hour Flow · Peak-Hour Factor · Level of Service · Congestion & Conflict Points

Project context

Assessing school-induced traffic in a congested urban setting

Site condition

South Point School and College is located beside Chowdhury Para Road near the DIT Road intersection. Its multiple school buildings and large student population generate concentrated movement during school start and end periods.

Assessment focus

The project examined road-network conditions, vehicle flows, pedestrian movement, parking, pick-up/drop-off activity, conflict points and stakeholder concerns before developing mitigation measures.

Methodology

Field-based traffic impact assessment

01 Reconnaissance survey

Observed adjacent land use, road characteristics, congestion, footpaths, school access and surrounding transport conditions.

02 Traffic & pedestrian counts

Traffic counts were conducted at three intersections in four 15-minute periods, alongside pedestrian counts at six locations.

03 Stakeholder interviews

Students, guardians, school authorities, traffic police and street vendors were consulted to document problems and possible solutions.

04 Traffic analysis

Traffic volume, mode share, peak-hour flow, peak-hour factor and road Level of Service were calculated and compared spatially.

Key findings

Congestion, access and pedestrian-safety challenges

Road performance
Persistent LOS F on Chowdhury Para

The right lane of Chowdhury Para Road was classified as LOS F in all four observed periods; DIT North-to-South also operated at E or F.

Vehicle mix
Cars, bikes & rickshaws dominate

These three modes together represented more than half of traffic on Chowdhury Para Road, with their combined share reaching 86% in one noon period.

Conflict points
School frontage concentrates conflicts

Pick-up/drop-off activity, street vendors and wrong-direction vehicle movement contributed to conflict locations around the school.

Pedestrian safety
Road crossing preferred over footbridge

During the five-minute observation, 85 people crossed using the road compared with 27 using the foot overbridge.

Study limitations

The project did not forecast future trip generation, seasonal variation or historical travel-pattern change. LOS was estimated from volume-capacity ratios rather than vehicle speed, and traffic counts covered selected 15-minute peak intervals rather than full peak-hour observation.

Proposed interventions

Site-specific measures to reduce school traffic impacts

01 · Access Separate drop-off and pick-up

Move motorized drop-off toward Matir Masjid Road and locate non-motorized drop-off on a local road beside the school.

02 · Parking Control on-street parking

Prohibit parking on Chowdhury Para Road, introduce penalties, and organize designated parking away from the school frontage.

03 · Walking Improve pedestrian facilities

Repair and widen footpaths, add ramps, discourage motorcycle use of footpaths and improve crossing conditions.

04 · Public transport Provide a bus stop & school bus

Create a designated bus stopping location near DIT-Chowdhury Para Road and introduce school-managed transport options.

05 · Intersection Redesign DIT-Chowdhury Para

Introduce corner widening, channelization, an at-grade pedestrian crossing and digital traffic signals.

06 · Street activity Relocate street vendors

Shift vendors from Chowdhury Para Road toward Matir Masjid Road and provide a school canteen as an alternative for students.

07 · Travel demand Encourage walking & car sharing

Promote walking and develop car-sharing arrangements for students, staff and guardians.

08 · Long term Plan for future development

Consider transport implications of the proposed MRT station and future land-use intensification, including possible school relocation.

Project outcome

The TIA connected observed traffic conditions and stakeholder concerns with a package of short- and long-term measures addressing access, parking, pedestrians, public transport, intersection design and travel-demand management.
