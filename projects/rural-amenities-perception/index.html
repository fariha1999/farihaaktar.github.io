Academic Project · Rural Planning · 2023

Exploring Variation in Farming and Non-Farming Communities' Perception on Urban Amenities in Rural Areas of Bangladesh

A rural-planning study comparing how farming and non-farming communities perceive the need for urban amenities in rural Bangladesh, and examining how socio-economic characteristics influence those perceptions.

Objective Identify socio-economic factors influencing farmers' and non-farmers' perceived need for urban amenities, and determine where the two communities differ in their priorities for rural development.
Study area 10 villages · Dhaka · Mymensingh · Rajshahi · Chittagong divisions
Sample 452 respondents · 182 farmers · 270 non-farmers · 22 FGDs · 24 KIIs
Methods & tools Questionnaire Survey · Likert Scale · Descriptive Statistics · Kruskal-Wallis H Test · Ordered Logistic Regression · R

Project context

Understanding rural development priorities from community perception

Research problem

Rural development programs can fail to reflect actual community priorities when the needs of farming and non-farming households are treated as identical. This study compared the two groups directly.

Amenity framework

Perception was assessed through amenity quality, accessibility, need for improvement, support to livelihood and adaptation level across education, health, infrastructure, economic services, community facilities and digital connectivity.

Methodology

Mixed community survey and statistical analysis

01 Village sampling

Ten villages from four divisions were selected, representing different rural contexts and varying proximity to urban centers, highways and rivers.

02 Primary data collection

452 respondents were surveyed through direct interviews, supplemented by 22 FGDs and 24 KIIs.

03 Perception measurement

Five-point Likert scales measured perceived importance, quality, accessibility, required improvement, livelihood support and adaptation.

04 Statistical modeling

Descriptive analysis, Kruskal-Wallis H tests and ordered logistic regression were implemented in R.

Key findings

Both groups value amenities, but their priorities differ

Overall need
3.90 vs 3.95 Both groups considered amenities important

Overall perceived-need scores were 3.90 for farmers and 3.95 for non-farmers on the five-point scale.

Sample
182 / 270 Farmers and non-farmers

The study directly compared 182 farming respondents with 270 non-farming respondents across the ten villages.

Farmer priorities
Growth centers Strong livelihood orientation

Farmers placed particularly high importance on growth centers and local markets because of their role in trading agricultural products.

Non-farmer priorities
Education · Economy · ICT Greater emphasis on diversified services

Non-farming communities gave greater importance to education, economic services and internet provision.

Study limitations

The sample covered specific villages in only four divisions, so the findings may not represent all rural areas of Bangladesh. In some cases respondents also could not provide precise information about existing infrastructure and amenities.

Significant differences

Five amenities varied significantly by farming status

p = 0.027 Education

Non-farmers placed comparatively greater importance on education than farmers.

p = 0.003 Growth center & local market

Farmers showed stronger dependence on markets and growth centers for agricultural livelihoods.

p = 0.003 Economic service

Both groups valued economic services, with non-farmers showing stronger emphasis in the model.

p = 0.009 Community space

Community space was important to both groups, particularly for social interaction and local development.

p = 0.013 ICT & internet

Non-farmers assigned greater importance to digital connectivity and internet provision.

Planning recommendations

Community-sensitive priorities for rural development

Quality education

Improve teacher quality, reduce teacher-student ratios, expand secondary access and strengthen technical, agricultural and ICT learning.

Growth centers & markets

Improve road access, regular maintenance, flood resilience, storage and processing facilities for rural market systems.

Economic services

Expand affordable microcredit, simplify government loan processes and improve access to banking agents, ATMs and seed finance.

Community space

Provide playgrounds, community centers, clubs and meeting spaces that strengthen social interaction and youth development.

ICT & internet

Improve broadband infrastructure, affordability and digital skills so rural communities can access education, healthcare and employment opportunities.

Project outcome

The study demonstrated that rural amenity planning should not assume a single set of priorities for all households. Farming and non-farming communities share a strong overall demand for better amenities, but differ meaningfully in how they value education, markets, economic services, community space and digital connectivity.
