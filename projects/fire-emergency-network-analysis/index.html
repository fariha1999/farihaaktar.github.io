A GIS-Based Network Analysis to Determine the Shortest Route and Service Area of Fire Stations to Hospitals for Emergency Response

An academic GIS project evaluating emergency accessibility between fire stations and tertiary hospitals in Dhaka City Corporation through service-area analysis, shortest-path routing and response-time assessment.

Objective Identify the service area of existing fire stations, determine shortest routes from fire stations to tertiary hospitals, and assess emergency response time across the Dhaka City Corporation network.
Study area Dhaka City Corporation
Facilities analyzed 15 fire stations · 23 tertiary hospitals
Tools & data ArcGIS 10.4 · Network Analyst · R · RAJUK road/land-use data · Sheltech DCC boundary
Spatial outputs
Emergency Response Network Analysis

Project context

Assessing emergency accessibility through the urban road network

Problem

Hospital fire emergencies require rapid access from nearby fire stations, but congestion, road-network conditions and the spatial distribution of emergency facilities can increase response time.

Analytical scope

The project focused on 23 tertiary-level hospitals and 15 fire stations within Dhaka City Corporation, using the road network to evaluate coverage and emergency routing.

Methodology

GIS-based service-area and shortest-path analysis

01 Prepare network data

Road, land-use and DCC boundary shapefiles were assembled and processed in ArcGIS.

02 Map emergency facilities

Fire stations and tertiary hospitals were selected, clipped to the study area and integrated with the road network.

03 Service-area analysis

Network Analyst was used to delineate accessible service areas using the study's response-time and travel-speed assumptions.

04 Route & response analysis

Shortest paths were generated and response times were compared statistically, including distribution and spatial-pattern assessment.

Key findings

Coverage gaps and response-time challenges

Coverage
6 of 23 Hospitals within service areas

Only six selected tertiary hospitals fell within the modeled service area of existing fire stations.

Mean response
23.50 min Above the reference standard

The mean modeled response time for the identified routes was substantially higher than the 9 min 20 sec reference used in the study.

Spatial disparity
DNCC > DSCC Greater access challenge in the north

Average response time was 22.94 minutes in DNCC and 19.36 minutes in DSCC, with particularly limited coverage in DNCC.

Study limitation

The project did not use field-observed real travel-time data and did not conduct least-cost analysis, so the modeled routes represent network-based analytical scenarios rather than real-time emergency routing.

Planning implications

Improving emergency fire response

Improve facility coverage

Assess new fire-station locations so that more tertiary hospitals fall within effective service areas.

Maintain route information

Fire stations should maintain shortest-route information while accounting for one-way streets, repairs and temporary blockages.

Strengthen emergency access

Dedicated communication and priority access for fire vehicles can help reduce delays during hospital emergencies.
